### 3.5.1 Game Flowchart / State Diagrams

The flow of the game is described at three levels of detail. The core game loop for one spoken
objective, which is prompt, listen, match, and act, is shown in Figure 3.1 and explained in
Section 3.2.2. The session flow, from opening the application to finishing a level, is shown in
Figure 3.2. The states of the listening panel, which is what the child sees while a word is live,
are shown in Figure 3.3.

**Main menu and nickname.** The application opens on the main menu, which carries four buttons:
ADVENTURE, MINI GAMES, SETTINGS, and ABOUT. MINI GAMES is drawn and visible but is deliberately
not connected to anything, because no mini game exists in this build, and a button that opened an
empty screen would be a worse answer than one that does not move. SETTINGS and ABOUT open panels
over the menu. ADVENTURE opens the nickname dialog, and the menu behind it is thrown out of focus
while the dialog is open. The name typed there is checked against the names already saved in the
online database, and a name that is already in use is reported with a free alternative filled in,
so that the records of two children can be told apart. OKAY saves the nickname and opens the
journey map. CANCEL closes the dialog and returns to the menu.

**The journey map.** The map is two boards rather than one. The first shows the places: Bahay,
Paaralan, Parke, and Palengke. A place is open once the player has reached its first step; a place
that is not yet open keeps its board but wears a padlock and cannot be pressed. Choosing an open
place shows the second board, which is the scenes inside that place as numbered tiles, with the
stars the scene was last finished with printed under each tile. A tile can be entered once the
player has reached its step and the scene behind it has been built. Pressing an open tile loads
that scene.

**One list for the whole journey.** The order of the journey, meaning every place marker and every
scene between them, is written down in one place, and both the map and the progress counter read
it, so the two cannot disagree about what comes next. The list holds twenty steps: four place
markers and sixteen scenes. Fifteen of those scenes are built and playable, and they are the
fifteen listed in Section 3.2.4. The sixteenth, in which Kylo leaves home for school, and the
whole of Palengke, are designed but not built. They still appear on the map, because the map is
meant to show where the journey goes and not only where the child has been, and progress steps
over them rather than stopping at them.

**Inside a scene.** Each scene runs its ordered list of objectives. For each objective the game
prompts, listens, matches, and acts. A match completes the objective and the next one begins after
the pacing delay described in Section 3.2.2. A non-match returns the game to listening with no
penalty. During a transition such as a door or a screen fade, the sequence is held so that nothing
can be answered behind the fade. Always-on words such as Talon (jump) can be said at any time.

**Help when a word will not come.** Repeated misses on the same word are answered in steps rather
than ignored. After two misses the word is said again, slowly, without the child having to ask for
it, which is the most important accessibility behaviour in the game, because a child who cannot
reach or does not understand the replay button still gets help. After four misses Teacher Rachel
points at the replay button. Ten seconds of silence brings an encouragement, and the whole prompt
is repeated every twenty seconds after that. Any speech, right or wrong, resets the silence.

**Finishing a scene.** When the scene is finished, the summary panel opens. It greets the child by
the nickname entered at the menu, names the place that was completed, and shows the points for the
scene, how many attempts it took, and a rating out of five stars earned on accuracy rather than
on speed. Finishing the scene at all earns the first star, so a child who reaches the end is never
shown nothing. Three buttons lead out of the panel: CONTINUE, which first shows the words the scene
taught and what each one means, and then opens the next scene; REPLAY, which runs the same scene
again; and the cross, which returns to the journey map. Whichever of the three is pressed, the
scene counts as finished and the next tile opens, because the child did reach the end and the
button pressed afterwards should not decide that. A rating is only ever replaced by a better one,
so going back to a scene already finished cannot cost the child the stars already earned.

**Finishing a level.** Bahay, Paaralan, and Parke each end on their fifth scene. There the totals
shown are the level's, gathered across all five of its scenes, and three further things happen. One
record is written to the online database for the whole level, so a record is written once per level
and not once per scene. The most recent records are then read back and listed on the level-complete
panel, together with the words practised during the level. The level's badge is awarded, and the
next place is opened on the map.

**What is saved, and where.** Two kinds of progress are kept on the device itself. The first is a
single number, the furthest step along the journey that has been reached, from which the locked and
unlocked state of every place and every tile is derived. The second is the star rating of each
scene, kept separately and by scene name, so that a child who goes back and does better keeps the
better rating without the journey moving. Progress is therefore saved per scene, and an adult can
stop after any scene without losing the child's work. The online database holds the level records
described above; it is not what the game reads to decide what is unlocked.

**The listening panel.** While a word is live, a plank under the microphone says what the game is
doing: SPEAK NOW, LISTENING, PROCESSING, TEACHER RACHEL, CORRECT, TRY AGAIN, or MIC IS OFF, and
nothing at all between words. These are the states shown in Figure 3.3. Two of them need
explanation. The recognizer is restarted after every result and also times out by itself when
nobody is speaking, so listening stops and starts every few seconds while a child is simply
thinking; shown literally, the plank would flicker between two states without end. A drop in
listening is therefore doubted for a short interval before it is believed, and no state may be
replaced until it has been on screen long enough to be read. The narrator is the exception: while
Teacher Rachel is speaking the microphone is deliberately deaf and the panel says so, because the
child genuinely should not be talking over her. An answer, right or wrong, is shown at once and
held long enough to be read before the next word is asked.

### 3.5.2 Wireframes and Screen Layouts

The screens of the game are listed below with their main elements. The wireframes are placed in
Appendix B and the final screenshots in Appendix F.

**Main menu.** The title, the illustrated room behind it, and the four buttons ADVENTURE, MINI
GAMES, SETTINGS, and ABOUT.

**Nickname dialog.** Opens over the main menu, which is thrown out of focus behind it. One text
field for the child's name, filled in with the name last used, a line that reports a name already
in use and offers a free one instead, and the OKAY and CANCEL buttons.

**Settings panel.** Opens over the main menu, and during play over the scene. Four rows: SOUND and
MUSIC as on and off plates, a slider for the sound effects, and a slider for Teacher Rachel's own
volume. Opening it during play pauses the scene.

**About panel.** Opens over the main menu and names the game and the proponents.

**Journey map, places board.** The four places, Bahay, Paaralan, Parke, and Palengke, each as a
board with its own picture. A place that is not yet open wears a padlock and cannot be pressed.

**Journey map, scenes board.** The scenes inside the chosen place, as numbered tiles along the
road, each with the stars it was last finished with printed underneath and a padlock on the tiles
that cannot yet be entered. A back arrow returns to the places board.

**Gameplay scene.** The illustrated situation with the playable character in it. Across the top are
the back button, the plank naming the place and level, and the menu button that opens the settings.
Teacher Rachel's plaque carries her portrait and the subtitle of the line she is speaking. The
signpost carries the word being asked for and what it means. Below them are the plank that reports
the listening state, the microphone, and the replay button, which repeats the instruction and,
pressed a second time, says the word slowly.

**Scene summary panel.** Opens when the scene is finished. A banner reading COMPLETE, or TRY AGAIN
on the lowest rating, the greeting with the child's name, the line naming the place that was
completed, the total points, the total attempts, and five stars. The buttons are CONTINUE, REPLAY,
and a cross that returns to the journey map.

**Words learned panel.** Opens from CONTINUE, before the next scene. One row for each Filipino word
the scene taught, with what that word means.

**Level complete panel.** Shown at the end of the fifth scene of a level. The player's name, the
words said correctly out of those asked, the number of voice attempts, the list of words practised
across the whole level, a rating in three stars, and the most recent records read back from the
online database.
