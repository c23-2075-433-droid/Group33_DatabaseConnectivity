## PURPOSE OF VALIDATION

This form is intended for the validation of the Filipino vocabulary words proposed for
implementation in the SALINLAHI capstone project. The selected words are organized according to
the game's three levels and their corresponding scenes.

The evaluator is requested to review the accuracy, spelling, meaning, pronunciation, and
contextual appropriateness of the proposed Filipino words for Grade 2 and Grade 3 learners.

**How the words are used.** In SALINLAHI the Filipino word is the control: the child must say the
word aloud for the character to act. Every word below is therefore one the learner is asked to
pronounce, not merely to recognize.

**Scope.** 16 scenes, 75 spoken prompts, 50 distinct Filipino words. Fifteen of the sixteen
scenes are already implemented. Level 1, Scene 6 is designed and planned but not yet built; it is
marked as such below, and the evaluator's comments on its words are welcome all the same.

**On repetition.** A word that appears in more than one scene is repeated deliberately, so that it
is practised again in a new setting rather than learned once and dropped. The repeated entries are
not duplications to be removed, though the evaluator's view on whether any word recurs too often,
or too soon after its last appearance, is welcome.

**On stress marks.** Stress and glottal stops are marked throughout this form as a pronunciation
reference. In the game itself, marks appear on screen only where the bare spelling would be a
different word:

| Shown to the child | means | not to be read as |
|---|---|---|
| TAYÔ | to stand up | táyo, we |
| UPÔ | to sit down | úpo, the bottle gourd |
| BUKÁS | to open | búkas, tomorrow |
| BÁSA | to read | basâ, wet |
| TALÓN | to jump | tálon, a waterfall |
| BÁTÌ | to greet | batî, beaten, as an egg is |

Every other word appears unmarked, in ordinary Filipino spelling, so that a mark on screen always
signals that the distinction matters. The evaluator is asked to confirm both this set and the
marks used throughout the form.
